# AI-Infrastructure Bottleneck — Investment Thesis

*Author: Ali Saleh Al Taher. The map (`build.py`) is the evidence; this is the view. Not advice.*

## The view: own the bottleneck, not the headline chip
The market pays up for the visible winner (the GPU). My thesis is that the **durable, defensible
economics sit one or two layers upstream**, at whichever link is physically supply-constrained and
slowest to add capacity. As each constraint is relieved with capital, scarcity — and pricing power —
**migrates upstream**. Today the binding constraint is **advanced packaging (CoWoS, tightness ~92)**
and **HBM memory (~87)**; the next leg is **power & grid (~82)**, which capital is only now reaching.

## Why this is non-consensus
Consensus narrative = "AI = buy NVIDIA." The bottleneck framing says NVIDIA's own deliveries are
**gated by the layers beneath it** (CoWoS, HBM), so the constraint owners capture rent with less
hype and, often, better risk/reward. The map makes the constraint **explicit and scoreable**
(utilisation, lead time, supply inelasticity, concentration) rather than a vibe.

## How it connects to the rest of my work
- **HBM is the bottleneck I can own cleanly** → that's the Micron memo (`../equity-valuation/THESIS_MU.md`).
- **Packaging (CoWoS)** is TSMC-controlled → expressed via diversified semis/foundry exposure.
- **Power & grid** is the *next* constraint → the migration thesis says watch it now, before it's
  the consensus trade.

## What would make me wrong
- **The constraint clears faster than expected** (capacity catches up; lead times fall) → rent
  competes away and the upstream premium compresses.
- **Demand disappoints** (AI capex digestion) → every layer de-rates together; "upstream" doesn't
  protect you in a demand shock, only in a supply-constrained boom.
- **My tightness inputs are wrong** — they are curated estimates, not measured telemetry. Replacing
  them with hard utilisation/lead-time data is the obvious next iteration and could re-rank layers.

## Signposts to track
CoWoS capacity-add announcements (TSMC) · HBM long-term-agreement pricing · grid interconnection
queue lengths and transformer/turbine lead times · hyperscaler capex guides (the demand tell).

## The investable conclusion
Be **long the tightest, most concentrated, slowest-to-respond layers** (HBM, packaging, then power),
sized to conviction in demand persistence, and **re-rank as capital migrates**. The map is meant to
be re-run quarterly — the edge is in spotting the *next* binding constraint before it's priced.
