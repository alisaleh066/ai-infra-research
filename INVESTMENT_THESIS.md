# Investment Thesis — one process, three expressions

*Author: Ali Saleh Al Taher. This ties the three tools to how I actually invest (thesis-driven,
sized, reconciled against live positions in a GBP Trading212 book). Not investment advice.*

## The through-line
I run a **systems-engineering process applied to markets**: break the problem down, build the model,
test the assumptions before trusting the answer. The unifying idea across all three projects is the
**AI build-out's returns accrue to physical bottlenecks and to real assets whose value is
manufactured, not bought.** Each tool is one expression of that, and they're meant to corroborate
each other.

```
   Bottleneck map ─────────────▶ WHERE is the binding constraint & where does capital migrate?
        │  (HBM is one of the tightest layers)
        ▼
   Equity valuation (MU) ──────▶ Is the cleanest listed owner of that bottleneck mispriced?
        │  (scenario DCF: bear $12 / base $60 / bull $127)
        ▼
   Real-estate ROI / waterfall ▶ Same discipline on a real asset: manufacture NOI, structure
                                  debt, check the LP wins after the promote and survives downside.
```

## The single sentence I'd defend in an interview
> *"I'm long the AI memory/packaging bottleneck. I own it cleanest through Micron, where my
> scenario DCF says the stock is an asymmetric bet on HBM tightness persisting — paid in the
> bull/base case, with a real cyclical downside I size for — and I apply the same assumption-driven,
> stress-tested discipline to real assets via an institutional LP/GP waterfall."*

## What would make the whole thesis wrong
- **AI capex digestion / demand shock** — the bottleneck premium and the memory cycle both depend on
  demand persisting; in a demand shock, "upstream" doesn't save you.
- **The constraints clear faster than expected** — capacity catches up, rent competes away.
- **Rates higher-for-longer** — hits the real-estate exit cap and the equity discount rate alike.
